01 About

I’m a machine learning researcher in the Financial Computing & Analytics Group at UCL, where I recently finished a First Class MEng in Computer Science. I’m interested in what happens when the structure we already know about financial data — dependence between firm characteristics, the shape of a limit order book — is built directly into a model, instead of being left for it to rediscover from noisy samples.

That has meant dependence-informed sparse networks for cross-sectional return prediction (ACM ICAIF 2026), a thesis with Prof. Tomaso Aste on how graph-aligned architectures and attention generalise in high dimensions, and short-horizon research on European natural-gas futures with Centiva Capital and A2A — where a key finding was that statistically measurable predictability doesn’t reliably survive spreads, latency and transaction costs.

Originally from Shenzhen, now in London. Away from the screen I’m usually behind a camera, or hunting for something good to eat.

02 Research

  1. Structure-aware learning

    Building known or estimated dependence — graphs, cliques, hierarchies — into network wiring and attention, so models learn from fewer samples and stay interpretable.

  2. Empirical asset pricing with ML

    Cross-sectional return prediction from firm characteristics, judged by honest out-of-sample ranking rather than in-sample fit.

  3. Market microstructure

    Short-horizon signals in limit order books, and the gap between statistical predictability and executable, cost-aware profit.
